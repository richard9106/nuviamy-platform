A copyable outline for therapy progress notes

Start with the structure your practice uses. This outline is an example of how to organize a note so you can review it and find the relevant context again.

  1. Session reference. Client record, appointment, author, and date.
  2. Session focus. The topic or goal addressed in the session.
  3. What happened. What the client reported and what the therapist observed or did. Keep those sources clear in the wording.
  4. Progress. What changed or remains open in relation to the relevant goal, when applicable.
  5. Follow-up. Agreed next steps and anything to revisit at the next appointment.

Fictional example

Session focus: Review how the agreed weekly routine worked.

What happened: The client reported trying the routine twice and identified a scheduling obstacle. The therapist and client discussed how the routine fit into their week.

Progress and follow-up: The client identified a more workable time. Review how that change went at the next session.

Before finalizing a note, verify the client and appointment, check that the content matches the session, and follow your practice’s review process. Try finding the same draft by client or appointment so you know where to pick up next time.
