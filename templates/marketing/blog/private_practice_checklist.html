Set up your practice before the first appointment

  1. Define who does what. Write down who handles booking requests, forms, notes, and invoices. If you work alone, use the same list to decide which tasks need a recurring calendar block.
  2. Configure your calendar. Set your time zone, working hours, appointment lengths, and buffers. Send a test confirmation and compare the time it shows with your calendar.
  3. Prepare the intake steps. List the forms you use, who sends them, and who reviews them. Give clients one place to find instructions and tell them what to do if they cannot complete a form online.
  4. Choose how to organize notes. Agree on note names, where drafts live, and when to review them. See the progress note outline for an example of a reusable structure.
  5. Decide how clients will pay. Set up the payment methods you plan to accept. Walk through how you will issue an invoice, mark it paid, and handle a cancellation or correction.
  6. Check who can see each item. Review staff roles and test the client view. In particular, check that client-facing messages contain only the information you intend to share.

Run a first-client rehearsal

Create a fictional client in a test workspace. In a separate browser session, open the invitation as that client, check the appointment time, and return a sample form. Back in the practice workspace, find the submission, create a draft note, and locate it from the client record. Use a test payment environment if you also rehearse payments.

Record where you paused or needed help. Assign each problem an owner and repeat the affected step after fixing it. Keep the rehearsal as a short checklist you can reuse when you change software or bring in a new teammate.
